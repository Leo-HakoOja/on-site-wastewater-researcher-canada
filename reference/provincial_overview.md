## National context — there is no single Canadian on-site code

On-site wastewater is regulated **provincially and territorially**, not
federally. There is no national on-site sewage code that all jurisdictions
adopt. Instead, each jurisdiction regulates under one of three kinds of statute:

- a **Public Health Act** (protecting health — e.g., BC, SK, NL, YT, NT, NU),
- an **Environment / Environmental Protection / Water Act** (protecting the
  environment — e.g., QC, MB, NS, PE), or
- a **Building Code / Safety Codes** framework (treating systems as
  construction — e.g., ON, AB).

What jurisdictions share is a set of **model codes and standards** they draw on:

- **National Building Code / National Plumbing Code of Canada** (NRC model
  codes) — provinces adopt/adapt these; plumbing up to the building drain is
  usually NPC-based, while on-site treatment/dispersal is provincial.
- **CSA B66-21** (9th ed., 2021) — *Design, material, and manufacturing
  requirements for prefabricated septic tanks and sewage holding tanks.*
  Referenced directly by several jurisdictions (e.g., Yukon mandates the
  current CAN/CSA-B66 for tank construction).
- **NSF/ANSI 40** — *Residential Wastewater Treatment Systems*; certifies
  **secondary** treatment units (ATUs), rated capacity 1,514–5,678 L/day
  (400–1,500 gal/day), Class I performance.
- **NSF/ANSI 245** — *Residential Wastewater Treatment Systems — Nitrogen
  Reduction*; same capacity range, requires ≥ 50% total-nitrogen reduction.
  Certifying to 245 also satisfies NSF/ANSI 40.
- **BNQ family (Québec / national)** — see Québec below. Note the correction:
  **BNQ 3680-905 covers prefabricated *septic tanks*, not advanced treatment.**
  Advanced/treatment-technology certification uses **NQ/BNQ 3680-910** (stand-
  alone treatment systems for isolated dwellings) and **CAN/BNQ 3680-600**
  (onsite residential wastewater treatment technologies, built on NSF 40
  methodology).

**Threshold note:** most "on-site" regimes apply below a **daily design-flow
cap**, above which separate municipal/larger-works rules take over. Confirmed
examples: BC's *Sewerage System Regulation* applies below 22,700 L/day
(5,000 Imp gal), with a Professional required above 9,100 L/day;
Saskatchewan public-health-inspector authority below 18 m³/day (18,000 L/day);
NL *Sanitation Regulations* below 4,546 L/day; PEI practitioner scope to 6,810
L/day (1,500 Igal/day).

## Provinces (west to east)

### British Columbia — **Confidence: High**
- **Instrument:** *Sewerage System Regulation* under the *Public Health Act*,
  supported by the **Sewerage System Standard Practice Manual (SPM), Version 3
  (September 2014)** — the sole version in force since January 1, 2016.
- **Administration:** regional **health authorities** under the Ministry of
  Health. BC uses a **registration/filing** model: an **Authorized Person**
  files documents certifying the system meets standards, rather than the AHJ
  issuing a prescriptive design permit.
- **Classification:** **Type 1** (septic tank only), **Type 2** (septic +
  secondary treatment), **Type 3** (secondary + disinfection).
- **Practitioner:** **Authorized Person (AP)** = a **Registered Onsite
  Wastewater Practitioner (ROWP)** (registered with **ASTTBC**) **or** a
  **Professional** (P.Eng./P.Geo.). ROWPs are limited to Type 1 and Type 2
  systems; only a **Professional** may handle **Type 3** systems and systems
  with an estimated minimum daily domestic flow **over 9,100 L/day**.
- **Scope cap:** the *Sewerage System Regulation* (**BC Reg 326/2004**) applies
  to systems with a design daily domestic flow **below 22,700 L/day** (5,000
  Imp gal); at or above that → **Municipal Wastewater Regulation** under the
  Environmental Management Act.

### Alberta — **Confidence: High**
- **Instrument:** **Private Sewage Systems Standard of Practice (SOP) 2021**
  (4th ed.), adopted under the **Safety Codes Act** (plumbing/private sewage
  discipline).
- **Administration:** **Safety Codes Council**; permits issued by accredited
  municipalities/agencies.
- **Practitioner:** certified installer/designer; training delivered through
  **AOWMA** (Alberta Onsite Wastewater Management Association).
- **Note:** the Alberta SOP is widely treated as a national benchmark, and
  AOWMA-style certification is recognized across much of Canada.
- **Distinctive system types.** Alberta recognizes several dispersal/system
  categories most other Canadian regimes do not:
  - **Open discharge systems (SOP §8.7)** — primary-treated effluent (Level
    1) released to ground surface through a designed outlet, with landscaping
    to contain and spread effluent on the property; must infiltrate within
    30 m (100 ft) of the discharge point. Soil-texture and subdivision
    restrictions apply (see below). Setbacks under §8.7.2.1: **50 m to a
    water well, 100 m to a licensed municipal well, 45 m to a watercourse,
    90 m to a property line, 45 m to a building.** Hard limit: not allowed
    on a quarter section where more than **4 parcels have been subdivided
    out** (existing systems may remain).
  - **Sewage lagoons (SOP Part 9)** — engineered evaporative/storage basins;
    setbacks for a single-family dwelling lagoon under §9.1.2.1: 100 m to a
    water well/source, 100 m to a municipal well, 90 m to a watercourse,
    30 m to a property line, 45 m to a building.
  - **LFH At-Grade Treatment Systems (SOP §8.6)** — forested-area dispersal
    that uses a natural leaf-litter (LFH) horizon and snow cover; requires
    Level 2 effluent.
  Open discharge in particular is unusual nationally and is one reason the
  Alberta SOP reads differently from other regimes — it explicitly addresses
  a range of dispersal methods for the rural/agricultural property profile.

### Saskatchewan — **Confidence: High**
- **Instrument:** **The Private Sewage Works Regulations** (Ministry of Health
  responsible), with **The Saskatchewan Onsite Wastewater Disposal Guide**
  (2nd ed., January 2009) setting design/installation requirements. Communities
  may add sewage-disposal bylaws under the **Public Health Act, 1994**. Related:
  **The Shoreland Pollution Control Regulations**.
- **Administration:** **public health inspectors** (Saskatchewan Health
  Authority) issue permits, inspect installations, and investigate complaints
  for systems under **18 m³/day** (18,000 L/day).
- **Industry:** **SOWMA** — Saskatchewan Onsite Wastewater Management
  Association. _(Correction: earlier draft said "WCOWMA-SK"; the provincial
  body is SOWMA.)_

### Manitoba — **Confidence: High**
- **Instrument:** **Onsite Wastewater Management Systems Regulation, M.R.
  83/2003**, under **The Environment Act**. Design/installation standards are in
  the **Supplementary Information Manual (2010)**.
- **Administration:** Manitoba **Environment and Climate Change**. Registration
  model; covers siting, installation, installer/hauler requirements, and
  decommissioning.

### Ontario — **Confidence: High**
- **Instrument:** the **Ontario Building Code (O. Reg. 332/12), Part 8 —
  Sewage Systems** (Division B), under the *Building Code Act*. Part 8 applies
  to the design, construction, operation, and maintenance of on-site sewage
  systems and to building near them. Larger systems fall under the Ministry of
  the Environment.
- **Administration:** the **principal authority** — a municipality, a **public
  health unit**, or a **conservation authority**, depending on the area.
- **Classification:** **Class 1–5** (Part 8, s.8.1.1.2 classification):
  - **Class 1** — non-water-carriage / privy-type: chemical, incinerating,
    recirculating, or self-contained portable toilet; privy vault, earth-pit or
    pail privy; composting toilet system.
  - **Class 2** — a **greywater** system (greywater/leaching pit; sink, bath,
    shower, laundry only — no toilet waste).
  - **Class 3** — a **cesspool** (receives all sewage with no pre-treatment;
    the most primitive full-disposal type still recognized).
  - **Class 4** — **septic tank + leaching bed**: the standard residential
    system (primary treatment in the tank, final treatment/disposal in the bed).
  - **Class 5** — a system using a **holding tank** for retention of hauled
    sewage at the site prior to collection by a hauled-sewage system (incl.
    temporary use / remediation where a Class 4 is impracticable).
- **Practitioner:** designers/installers qualify under the Building Code and
  hold a **BCIN** (Building Code Identification Number). Industry support via
  **OOWA** (Ontario Onsite Wastewater Association).

### Québec — **Confidence: High**
- **Instrument:** **Regulation respecting waste water disposal systems for
  isolated dwellings (CQLR c Q-2, r. 22)** under the *Environment Quality Act*.
- **Administration:** **municipalities** issue permits and enforce; provincial
  oversight by the environment ministry — **MELCCFP** (*Ministère de
  l'Environnement, de la Lutte contre les changements climatiques, de la Faune
  et des Parcs*; current name as of 2026).
- **Advanced systems:** all advanced-secondary and tertiary treatment systems
  must be **BNQ-certified** before use — under **NQ/BNQ 3680-910** (stand-alone
  treatment systems for isolated dwellings) and/or **CAN/BNQ 3680-600** (onsite
  residential treatment technologies). A product complies only if the
  manufacturer holds a current BNQ certificate and the product bears the BNQ
  label.
- **Note:** distinct from anglophone-province regimes; relies on prescribed
  system types matched to soil permeability and site classification.

### New Brunswick — **Confidence: High**
- **Instrument:** **On-site Sewage Disposal System Regulation — NB Reg
  2009-137** under the **Public Health Act**, applied with the **New Brunswick
  Technical Guidelines for On-site Sewage Disposal Systems (Version 6)**.
- **Administration:** **Department of Justice and Public Safety** (Health
  Protection Services / Technical Inspection Services). _(Correction: earlier
  draft put this under Health/Service NB.)_
- **Practitioner:** installation requires a **licensed installer**; licensing
  requires completing the **NB On-site Sewage Disposal Systems Provincial
  Installer Course** (offered annually).

### Nova Scotia — **Confidence: High**
- **Instrument:** **On-site Sewage Disposal Systems Regulations — N.S. Reg.
  194/2007**, consolidated to **N.S. Reg. 213/2018**, under the **Environment
  Act**.
- **Administration:** **Nova Scotia Environment and Climate Change** certifies
  practitioners and approves systems.
- **Practitioner roles** (per NSE) — four recognized roles:
  - **Professional Engineers** (licensed via Engineers Nova Scotia) — select or
    design systems per the Standard, or seek approval for non-standard designs.
  - **Qualified Persons (QPs)** — certified by NSE; select **pre-designed**
    systems from the Standard. (The Regulations define QP **levels**, e.g.,
    QP1/Level 1 at s.25(1)(a); the exact tier-by-tier scope is set in the
    Regulations.)
  - **Installers** — certified contractors who build the systems.
  - **Septic Tank Cleaners** — certified to pump, clean, and maintain systems.
  Industry support via **NSOWA**.

### Prince Edward Island — **Confidence: High**
- **Instrument:** **Sewage Disposal Systems Regulations** are now made under the
  **Water Act (Chapter W-1.1)** — current regulation **PEI Reg EC504/21**, in
  force **June 16, 2021**, when the Water Act and its supporting regulations
  replaced the prior Environmental Protection Act framework (EC625/13 / EC403/03).
- **Administration:** PEI **Department of Environment, Energy and Climate
  Action** (current name ⚠ VERIFY — provincial departments rename frequently).
- **Practitioner:** a **licensed septic contractor** (or a **registered
  installer** in their employ) must be on-site during installation; licensing
  requires an approved seminar + exam, or an equivalent out-of-province licence.
  **Licensed contractors / site assessors** may assess and select systems up to
  **6,810 L/day (1,500 Igal/day)**, Categories I–IV; larger systems require a
  **consulting engineer** licensed in PEI.

### Newfoundland & Labrador — **Confidence: Medium–High**
- **Instrument:** **Sanitation Regulations (CNLR 803/96)**, originally made
  under the **Public Health Act**, governing on-site sewage with a daily flow
  **< 4,546 L**. Practical requirements are in the **Private Sewage Disposal and
  Water Supply Standards** document. **Note:** NL replaced the old *Public
  Health Act* with the **Public Health Protection and Promotion Act (SNL 2018,
  c P-37.3)**, in force July 1, 2019. ⚠ VERIFY whether CNLR 803/96 continues
  under the new Act's transitional provisions or has been replaced — confirm
  against the current consolidation before citing.
- **Administration:** the **Department of Health and Community Services** owns
  the legislation; **Environmental Health Officers** enforce it, and **Service
  NL / Government Service Centre (GSC)** handles septic approvals and registers
  designers.
- **Practitioner:** plot plans and site evaluation must be prepared by an
  **Approved Designer** registered with the GSC; the septic tank + subsurface
  absorption field remains the standard system.

## Territories

> Territorial context differs sharply: **permafrost**, short thaw seasons, and
> dispersed communities make conventional soil dispersal difficult. Many
> communities rely on **trucked (haul) service** to holding tanks rather than
> in-ground systems, and municipal/community bodies carry much of the
> responsibility.

### Yukon — **Confidence: High**
- **Instrument:** **Sewage Disposal Systems Regulation (O.I.C. 1999/082)** under
  the **Public Health and Safety Act** (in force June 1, 1999), with **Design
  Specifications for Sewage Disposal Systems**.
- **Administration:** **Environmental Health Services** (Yukon government) — a
  **permit** is required before construction/installation/substantial repair,
  and **written EHS approval** before use.
- **Confirmed specifics:** untreated sewage may not be discharged to ground or
  water; a septic/holding tank must be **≥ 15 m** from any potable water
  source; **no system within 60 m** of a community well; tanks must conform to
  the current **CAN/CSA-B66**.

### Northwest Territories — **Confidence: Medium**
- **Instrument:** **Public Health Act (SNWT 2007, c 17)** with **General
  Sanitation Regulations**; on-site/holding-tank practice is shaped heavily at
  the **community** level, with engineering guidance in **MACA's "Good
  Engineering Practice for Northern Water and Sewer Systems."**
- **Administration:** **Health and Social Services** — **Environmental Health
  Officers (EHOs)** (certified public health inspectors based in Yellowknife,
  Hay River, and Inuvik, delegated as Public Health Officers by the Chief Public
  Health Officer under the Public Health Act) handle on-site sewage approvals;
  **Municipal and Community Affairs (MACA)** provides engineering guidance for
  community water/sewer infrastructure.

### Nunavut — **Confidence: Medium**
- **Instrument:** **Public Health Act (SNu 2016, c 13)** with **General
  Sanitation Regulations** (consolidated). ⚠ VERIFY any on-site-specific
  provisions.
- **Administration:** Department of **Health** plus **municipal corporations**,
  which hold full authority for water and sewage service delivery.
- **Practice:** predominantly **trucked water and sewage** (subsidy benchmark
  ~90 L/capita/day trucked vs. ~225 L/capita/day piped); in-ground on-site
  dispersal is uncommon due to permafrost.

## Cross-cutting comparison themes (for the assignment)

1. **Statutory basis** — health (BC, SK, NL, YT, NT, NU) vs. environment (QC,
   MB, NS, PE) vs. building code (ON, AB), and how that shapes who enforces
   (health inspector vs. building official vs. environment officer).
2. **Permit model** — prescriptive design permit (most) vs. **registration /
   filing by an Authorized Person** (BC) vs. municipal permitting (QC).
3. **Classification logic** — effluent-quality tiers (BC Type 1/2/3) vs.
   system-category classes (ON Class 1–5) vs. prescribed-system matching (QC) vs.
   capacity categories (PE I–IV).
4. **Practitioner certification** — ROWP (BC), AOWMA-trained (AB), public-health
   inspector permitting (SK), BCIN (ON), Qualified Person (NS), licensed
   installer + provincial course (NB), licensed contractor/site assessor (PE),
   Approved Designer (NL), P.Eng. above flow caps (several).
5. **Flow-cap thresholds** — the daily-flow line where on-site rules hand off to
   municipal/engineered regimes (BC ~22,700 / 9,100 L/day; SK 18,000; PE 6,810;
   NL 4,546).
6. **Environmental-sensitivity tools** — nitrogen reduction (NSF/ANSI 245),
   BNQ-certified advanced treatment (QC), disinfection (BC Type 3), setbacks
   near wells/surface water (e.g., YT 15 m / 60 m).
7. **North vs. south** — soil-dispersal regimes vs. permafrost / holding-tank /
   trucked-service realities in the territories.
