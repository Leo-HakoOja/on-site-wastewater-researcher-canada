# Glossary — On-Site Wastewater Terms

_Last updated: 2026-05-29_

Definitions are written as **engineering practice** (broadly true across the
field) unless noted. Jurisdiction-specific legal definitions live in
`provincial_overview.md` and may differ from these. Metric units throughout.

---

## System types and scope

**On-site wastewater system** — A system that treats and disperses wastewater on
the property where it is generated, rather than conveying it to a centralized
municipal sewage plant. Also called **decentralized**, **private sewage**, or
**individual sewage disposal** systems. Typically serves a single dwelling or
small cluster.

**Private sewage system (PSS)** — Common Canadian regulatory term for an on-site
system serving a private property. Used prominently in Alberta.

**Decentralized wastewater system** — Umbrella term covering on-site systems and
small cluster/community systems not connected to a large central plant.

**Design flow (design daily flow)** — The volume of wastewater per day the
system is sized for. Usually derived from the number of bedrooms (a proxy for
occupancy and peak load), fixture count, or estimated occupants. The single most
important sizing input.

**Hydraulic load** — The volume of wastewater applied to a component or to the
soil over time. Distinct from **organic load** (the strength of the wastewater,
measured as BOD/TSS).

---

## Treatment levels

**Primary treatment** — Physical separation of solids from liquid. In on-site
systems this is the **septic tank**: solids settle as sludge, fats/oils float as
scum, and clarified liquid (**effluent**) flows out. Removes settleable solids
but little dissolved or pathogenic load.

**Secondary treatment** — Biological treatment that reduces BOD and TSS to a
much lower level than a septic tank alone. Achieved by **sand/media filters**,
**aerobic treatment units (ATUs)**, or other packaged units.

**Tertiary (advanced) treatment** — Further treatment beyond secondary: nutrient
removal (nitrogen, phosphorus), additional filtration, and/or **disinfection**
(UV or chlorination). Required where receiving conditions are sensitive.

---

## Components

**Septic tank** — A buried, watertight tank providing primary treatment.
Compartments and an outlet **effluent filter** keep solids from leaving. Sized
by working (liquid) capacity. Prefabricated tanks in Canada are commonly
referenced to **CSA B66-21** (9th ed., 2021 — materials/construction); Québec
also uses **BNQ 3680-905** for residential prefabricated septic tanks.

**Effluent filter** — A screen at the septic tank outlet that blocks solids from
passing to the dispersal field, protecting the field from clogging.

**Distribution box (d-box)** — A junction that splits septic tank effluent
evenly among multiple dispersal trenches in a gravity system.

**Dosing / pump chamber** — A tank with a pump or siphon that delivers effluent
to the dispersal area in timed or volume-controlled **doses**, rather than
continuous trickle. Enables pressure distribution and even loading.

**Pressure distribution** — Effluent pumped through small-diameter, perforated
laterals so it is applied evenly across the entire dispersal area. Contrasts
with **gravity distribution**, which relies on slope and tends to overload the
near end.

**Lateral** — A perforated distribution pipe within the dispersal field.

## Dispersal / treatment field

**Treatment field / dispersal field / drainfield / leaching bed** — The
soil-based area where effluent is applied and final treatment + disposal occurs
as effluent percolates through unsaturated soil. (Terminology varies by
jurisdiction; "leaching bed" is Ontario usage, "treatment field" is Alberta
usage.)

**Trench** — A narrow excavation containing distribution pipe and aggregate (or a
proprietary chamber), through which effluent infiltrates the soil sidewalls and
bottom.

**Bed** — A wider excavation than a trench; generally less efficient per unit
area because it relies mostly on the bottom infiltrative surface.

**At-grade system** — Dispersal placed at the original ground surface under
imported fill, used where soil is suitable but shallow.

**Mound system** — An engineered sand mound built **above** native soil, used
where the water table or a restrictive layer is too shallow for an in-ground
field. Effluent is dosed under pressure into the sand, treated as it moves down,
then disperses into native soil.

**Sand filter** — A bed of specified sand that provides secondary treatment.
**Intermittent** filters dose once-through; **recirculating** filters pass
effluent through repeatedly for higher treatment.

**Drip dispersal** — Pressurized small-diameter tubing with emitters that
distributes treated effluent at shallow depth over a large area.

**Aerobic treatment unit (ATU)** — A packaged unit that introduces air to
support aerobic bacteria, producing higher-quality effluent than a septic tank.
Commonly certified to **NSF/ANSI 40** (secondary treatment) or **NSF/ANSI 245**
(nitrogen reduction); in Québec, to **CAN/BNQ 3680-600 / NQ 3680-910**. Rated
residential capacity range is 1,514–5,678 L/day (400–1,500 gal/day).

**Holding tank** — A watertight tank with no dispersal; wastewater is stored and
periodically pumped out and hauled away. A last-resort option where treatment/
dispersal is not feasible.

**Sewage lagoon** — An open earthen basin providing treatment via storage,
settling, and biological action; used on larger rural lots in some Prairie
jurisdictions.

## Site evaluation

**Site evaluation / site assessment** — The field investigation of a property to
determine what system the soil and site can support: soil profile, restrictive
layers, water table, slope, and separation constraints.

**Soil texture** — The proportion of sand, silt, and clay, classified into
texture groups (often USDA-style). Controls how fast water moves through soil.

**Soil structure** — How soil particles aggregate (e.g., granular, blocky,
massive). Affects permeability independently of texture.

**Percolation test ("perc test")** — A field test measuring how fast water
drains from a soaked test hole, expressed as minutes per unit drop (e.g.,
min/cm or min/inch). Historically the basis for sizing; many jurisdictions now
prefer or require a **soil morphology / texture** approach.

**Loading rate (soil application rate / hydraulic loading rate)** — The volume
of effluent applied per unit area of infiltrative surface per day (e.g.,
L/m²/day), set by soil type and effluent quality. Lower for tight soils, higher
for permeable soils.

**Linear loading rate** — Volume applied per unit *length* of dispersal field
(L/m/day), used to limit groundwater **mounding** on sloping or layered sites.

**Restrictive (limiting) layer** — A soil horizon that water cannot readily pass
through (bedrock, dense till, hardpan, high clay). Sets the bottom of usable
treatment soil.

**Seasonal high water table (SHWT)** — The highest level groundwater reaches
during the year (often spring). Often identified by **redoximorphic features**
(mottling) in the soil. Critical for vertical separation.

**Vertical separation** — The depth of unsaturated, suitable soil between the
bottom of the dispersal field and the nearest restrictive layer or seasonal high
water table. This soil column does the final treatment; minimum required depth
is a key jurisdiction-specific rule.

**Groundwater mounding** — Localized rise of the water table beneath a heavily
loaded dispersal area; managed via linear loading rate and field geometry.

## Separation distances and siting

**Separation distance (setback / clearance)** — The minimum required horizontal
distance between a system component and a feature to be protected (water well,
surface water, property line, building, slope/cut bank). Values are
jurisdiction-specific legal requirements.

**Exclusion area** — Land made unusable for dispersal because it falls within a
required setback from a protected feature.

---

## Effluent quality parameters

**BOD (biochemical oxygen demand)** — A measure of the organic strength of
wastewater; how much oxygen microbes need to break it down. High in raw sewage,
reduced by treatment.

**TSS (total suspended solids)** — The mass of suspended particles in the
effluent. Reduced by settling and filtration.

**Fecal coliform / E. coli** — Indicator organisms used to gauge pathogen load
and the effectiveness of disinfection.

**Nitrogen (total N)** — A nutrient of concern for **groundwater** (nitrate is a
drinking-water hazard). Hard to remove without advanced treatment; drives
setbacks and advanced-treatment requirements near sensitive aquifers.

**Phosphorus** — A nutrient of concern for **surface water** (eutrophication).
Soil adsorbs phosphorus well, so it is more of a concern near lakes/streams.

## People and oversight

**Authority Having Jurisdiction (AHJ)** — The body that administers and enforces
the applicable rules and issues permits: a provincial agency, a regional health
authority, a municipality, or a delegated certification body, depending on the
jurisdiction.

**Certified / authorized practitioner** — The licensed person permitted to
design, install, or inspect on-site systems. Title and certification path vary
by jurisdiction: BC **Authorized Person** = **ROWP** (registered with ASTTBC) or
**Professional**; Alberta **AOWMA-trained** certified installer/designer;
Ontario qualification via **BCIN**; Nova Scotia **Qualified Person (QP1/QP2)**;
New Brunswick **licensed installer**; PEI **licensed septic contractor / site
assessor**; Newfoundland & Labrador **Approved Designer**. See
`provincial_overview.md` for the full breakdown.

**Permit / authorization** — The legal approval to install a system, issued by
the AHJ after review of the design and site evaluation.
