# Key Sources — Annotated

_Last updated: 2026-05-29_

Authoritative starting points for researching on-site wastewater in Canada,
grouped by type. **Primary sources** (regulations, standards) outrank
**secondary sources** (manuals, textbooks, association material). When a claim
needs to be defended in graded work, cite the primary source.

> URLs are not listed here on purpose — government regulation URLs change and
> editions are revised. Search each source by its **official title + issuing
> body + "current/consolidated"** to land on the in-force version. Confirm the
> **edition/year** before citing. Items not yet confirmed are marked ⚠ VERIFY.

---

## 1. National model codes & standards (shared baseline)

- **National Building Code of Canada (NBC)** and **National Plumbing Code of
  Canada (NPC)** — National Research Council / Canadian Commission on Building
  and Fire Codes. Model codes provinces adopt or adapt. Relevant mostly for
  building drains/plumbing up to the system; on-site treatment is provincial.
- **CSA B66-21** (9th ed., 2021) — *Design, material, and manufacturing
  requirements for prefabricated septic tanks and sewage holding tanks.*
  Materials/construction for manufactured tanks; referenced directly by several
  regimes (e.g., Yukon mandates the current CAN/CSA-B66). _Confirmed
  2026-05-29._
- **NSF/ANSI 40** — *Residential Wastewater Treatment Systems* — certification
  for **secondary** treatment units (ATUs); rated capacity 1,514–5,678 L/day
  (400–1,500 gal/day), Class I performance. _Confirmed 2026-05-29._
- **NSF/ANSI 245** — *Residential Wastewater Treatment Systems — Nitrogen
  Reduction* — requires ≥ 50% total-nitrogen reduction; same capacity range;
  certifying to 245 also satisfies NSF/ANSI 40. _Confirmed 2026-05-29._
- **BNQ family (Bureau de normalisation du Québec)** — _Correction (2026-05-29):
  **BNQ 3680-905 is the prefabricated **septic tank** standard, not advanced
  treatment.** Québec's advanced/treatment-technology certification (referenced
  by Q-2, r. 22) runs through **NQ/BNQ 3680-910** (stand-alone treatment
  systems for isolated dwellings) and **CAN/BNQ 3680-600** (onsite residential
  treatment technologies, built on NSF 40 methodology); **BNQ 3682-925** is the
  septic-tank certification protocol.

## 3. Authoritative secondary references

- **US EPA — Onsite Wastewater Treatment Systems Manual** (EPA/625/R-00/008,
  rev. February 2002; Office of Water + Office of Research and Development).
  _Confirmed 2026-05-29._ The most widely cited engineering reference for
  on-site design fundamentals in North America; explains soil treatment,
  hydraulic/organic loading, and technology selection. An update of the 1980
  *Design Manual: Onsite Wastewater Treatment and Disposal Systems*. Useful for
  the *why*, not for Canadian legal requirements.
- **Crites & Tchobanoglous — *Small and Decentralized Wastewater Management
  Systems*** (McGraw-Hill, 1998; ISBN 0-07-289087-8). _Confirmed 2026-05-29 —
  single edition._ Standard graduate-level textbook on decentralized wastewater
  engineering.
- **Provincial design manuals / standard practice manuals** (e.g., BC SPM) —
  bridge between the regulation and field practice; often the most useful
  practical document.

Treat association material as orientation and practice context, not as primary

- **WCOWMA** — Western Canada Onsite Wastewater Management Association
  (provincial bodies/chapters in the West; **AOWMA** is the Alberta body).
- **SOWMA** — Saskatchewan Onsite Wastewater Management Association _(the SK

- **NSOWA** — Nova Scotia Onsite Wastewater Association.
- **CIPHI** — Canadian Institute of Public Health Inspectors (relevant where
  health inspectors are the AHJ).

## 5. How to vet a source (quick rule)

1. **Primary over secondary** — regulation/standard beats a manual beats a
   blog.
2. **Current edition** — confirm the in-force version; on-site rules are
   revised regularly.
3. **Right jurisdiction** — a source is only authoritative for the jurisdiction
   that issued it.
4. **Issuing body** — prefer the government/standards body's own publication
   over a third-party reproduction.
5. **Record it** — when you confirm a ⚠ item, note the exact title, edition,
   section, and where you found it, then clear the flag.
